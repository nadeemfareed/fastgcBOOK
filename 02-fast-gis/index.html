    Chapter 3 — FAST-GIS: LiDAR survey sampling, plot extraction, and downstream products

    Software scope: FAST-GC 0.2.1, fastgc gis and fastgc --workflow plots-run. Demonstration: BR01 Airborne Laser Scanning (ALS) survey, five randomly selected circular plots, Plot 005 visual examples. Reproducibility status: the five-plot unbuffered clipping run and its downstream raster-mask failure were observed in terminal logs. A buffered Plot 005 clipping metadata file supplied with the figures was independently inspected: point_extent=core_plus_buffer, buffer_m=5, core_points=79,211, points_written=162,139. The uploaded rasters demonstrate downstream product existence; a complete successful terminal log for all five buffered plots was not supplied. The two standalone elevation/height GeoTIFF labels remain provisional.

    3.1 Scope and scientific motivation

    FAST-GIS bridges a georeferenced LiDAR survey and repeatable plot-level analysis. It discovers survey files, resolves survey geometry and coordinate reference systems (Coordinate Reference System (CRS)), selects or imports plot footprints, clips LAS/LAZ points, publishes a machine-readable collection, and hands that collection to FAST-GC for ground classification and forest/terrain products. Plot core and processing buffer are distinct: processing uses extra spatial context, while mapped raster results are masked back to the core polygon.

    FAST-GIS is not a ground classifier or canopy-height estimator. Those operations are performed by downstream FAST-GC workflows. This distinction is important for provenance, especially when rough-height sampling is used before ground classification.

    Scope boundary: FAST-GIS supports all five shapes for the geometry/import/continuous paths, but the current rough_height_stratified CLI explicitly restricts its shape to circle. Do not assume identical parameter behavior across modes.

    3.2 Plot geometry and processing footprint

    The plot core is the intended measurement area. A processing buffer of width surrounds it; its points provide neighborhood context for classification and raster interpolation.

    Geometry Definition / formula What it measures Typical use
    Circle Circular area specified by centre and radius Fixed-radius area Inventory plots, local forest structure
    Square Four equal sides and right angles Equal-sided area Grid-compatible sampling
    Rectangle Four sides with specified width and height Elongated survey area Transects and edge studies
    Ellipse Oval specified by major and minor dimensions Directional oval area Elongated features
    Regular hexagon Six equal sides Compact six-sided area Spatially regular tessellation

    For a circular core with a 15 m radius, the nominal area is 706.86 square metres. A 5 m buffer expands the processing radius to 20 m and the nominal processing area to 1,256.64 square metres. These are geometric areas, not guarantees of observed point coverage.

    Boundary interpretation: Point clouds used during processing can extend beyond the 15 m core. Core-masked raster products should not be mistaken for rectangular full-extent outputs or for a 20 m measurement plot.

    3.3.1 Random sampling: complete BR01 example

    Random sampling uses an observed-coverage grid to identify eligible centres, avoids overlaps between processing footprints, and uses a seed for reproducibility. The selected centres are therefore constrained by survey coverage rather than uniformly sampled from every coordinate inside the survey bounding box.

    conda activate fastgc

fastgc gis `
    --in_path "G:\ALS_Jin\concurrent\BR01\ALS\ALS-on_BR01_2019-07-05_300m.laz" `
    --out_dir "G:\FASTGC_BR01_Buffered_Test" `
    --sensor_mode ALS `
    --sampling random `
    --plots 5 `
    --shape circle `
    --radius 15 `
    --buffer 5 `
    --seed 42
    Parameter Function / definition Configuration guidance
    --in_path Source LAS/LAZ file or survey directory Use georeferenced source data; here BR01 ALS
    --out_dir New published FAST-GIS workspace Choose a new directory; the sampler publishes transactionally
    --sensor_mode Sensor type ALS, Uncrewed Aerial Laser Scanning (ULS), or Terrestrial Laser Scanning (TLS); here ALS
    --sampling Selection strategy random, continuous, imported, rough_height_stratified
    --plots Requested plot count Here 5; default 30; placement may fail if coverage is insufficient
    --shape Core polygon geometry Here circle; defaults to hexagon
    --radius Core radius, metres Here 15 m; default 15 m
    --buffer Extra processing support, metres Here 5 m; default 5 m; recommended for this core-masking example
    --seed Random-number seed Here 42; default 42
    --coverage_cell_m XY occupancy-cell size Default 5 m; smaller cells can improve footprint screening at additional cost
    --chunk_size LAS points read per chunk Default 500,000; adjust for available memory

    Observed evidence: A five-plot, zero-buffer run completed clipping in 9.76 s for the 12,531,758-point BR01 input, but subsequent plots-run stopped at raster masking. The uploaded buffered Plot 005 metadata establishes that the 5 m buffer configuration creates the expected core_plus_buffer contract. Do not present the 9.76 s timing as a benchmark for the buffered run.

    3.4 Continuous/systematic plot sampling

    The continuous sampler searches a spatial lattice of candidate plot centres and evaluates layout against observed XY coverage. --overlap_m specifies intended overlap between adjacent core footprints; --phase_steps controls lattice-offset search. Its purpose is regular spatial coverage, not a statistically independent random sample.

    fastgc gis `
    --in_path "G:\ALS_Jin\concurrent\BR01\ALS\ALS-on_BR01_2019-07-05_300m.laz" `
    --out_dir "G:\FASTGC_BR01_Continuous" `
    --sensor_mode ALS `
    --sampling continuous `
    --shape square `
    --width_m 30 `
    --height_m 30 `
    --overlap_m 5 `
    --buffer 5 `
    --phase_steps 10
    Parameter Function / definition Configuration guidance
    --sampling continuous Systematic layout selection Use for spatially distributed, regular plot coverage
    --shape square Plot footprint Circle, hexagon, square, rectangle, ellipse supported by geometry module
    --width_m Core width Default 30 m; controls square/rectangle/ellipse geometry
    --height_m Core height Default 30 m; used for rectangle/ellipse; square enforces equal sides
    --overlap_m Core-footprint overlap Default 5 m; must be nonnegative
    --buffer Additional processing context Default 5 m
    --phase_steps Candidate lattice phases Default 10; higher values can increase layout-search work
    --rotation_deg Footprint rotation Default 0°; relevant for supported continuous layouts
    --coverage_cell_m Observed occupancy grid resolution Default 5 m; trade-off between detail and cost

    Validation: This is a source-supported CLI example, not a run demonstrated by the uploaded BR01 outputs. It should be tested before attaching measured timing or plot-count claims.

    3.5 Rough-height-stratified circular sampling

    This mode samples low-, medium-, and high-rough-height plots before ground classification. It requires an existing DSM and a single LAS/LAZ source. The rough-height proxy subtracts a local low-return elevation estimate from the digital surface model and sets negative results to zero. This proxy is not a validated canopy-height model. The sampler classifies each candidate plot by the median valid rough-height value inside its circular core, and allocates an equal number of plots to three strata. The requested plot count must therefore be divisible by three.

    Step A — Create the required DSM

    fastgc `
    --workflow run `
    --in_path "G:\ALS_Jin\concurrent\BR01\ALS\ALS-on_BR01_2019-07-05_300m.laz" `
    --out_dir "G:\FASTGC_BR01_DSM" `
    --sensor_mode ALS `
    --products FAST_DSM `
    --grid_res 1.0
    Parameter Function / definition Configuration guidance
    --workflow run Standard source-cloud workflow Used here to generate the DSM
    --products FAST_DSM Digital surface model Rough-height sampling requires an existing DSM GeoTIFF
    --grid_res Raster cell size, metres Here 1 m; finer resolution increases raster cells and memory
    --out_dir Product output directory DSM must be accessible to the subsequent GIS command

    Step B — Select three plots per rough-height stratum

    fastgc gis `
    --in_path "G:\ALS_Jin\concurrent\BR01\ALS\ALS-on_BR01_2019-07-05_300m.laz" `
    --out_dir "G:\FASTGC_BR01_RoughHeight" `
    --sensor_mode ALS `
    --sampling rough_height_stratified `
    --dsm_path "G:\FASTGC_BR01_DSM\FAST_DSM\ALS-on_BR01_2019-07-05_300m.tif" `
    --plots 9 `
    --shape circle `
    --radius 15 `
    --buffer 5 `
    --low_max 5 `
    --medium_max 15 `
    --seed 42
    Parameter Function / definition Configuration guidance
    --dsm_path Existing DSM GeoTIFF Required; DSM and LAS must have matching embedded projected CRS
    --plots Total stratified sample Positive multiple of 3; here 9
    --shape circle Stratified core geometry Only circle supported by this mode
    --low_max Low/medium threshold in rough-height metres Default 5 m; adjust for vegetation height distribution
    --medium_max Medium/high threshold Default 15 m; must exceed low_max
    --radius, --buffer Core radius and processing support Defaults 15 m and 5 m
    --seed Reproducible candidate ordering Default 42

    Known limitation from the earlier BR01 attempt: LAS CRS WKT and DSM EPSG:25832 were not considered equal by the sampler's strict CRS comparison, even though both described ETRS89 / UTM zone 32N. The command above is illustrative, not validated on this specific input/DSM pair. Do not silently alter CRS metadata or describe a failed attempt as a successful demonstration.

    3.6 Imported plot definitions

    FAST-GIS accepts pre-existing plot polygons or centres from KML, KMZ, shapefile, GeoPackage, GeoJSON, and CSV. Vector coordinates must be interpretable in a known CRS; CSV centres may need --source_crs.

    fastgc gis `
    --in_path "G:\ALS_Jin\concurrent\BR01\ALS\ALS-on_BR01_2019-07-05_300m.laz" `
    --out_dir "G:\FASTGC_BR01_Imported" `
    --sensor_mode ALS `
    --sampling imported `
    --plot_file "G:\Survey\BR01_plots.geojson" `
    --buffer 5
    Parameter Function / definition Configuration guidance
    --sampling imported Use externally defined plot geometries No random selection; footprints come from plot file
    --plot_file Input plot-definition file KML/KMZ/SHP/GPKG/GeoJSON/CSV; example path must exist
    --layer Layer selector Supply for a multilayer GeoPackage when necessary
    --source_crs CRS override For missing LAS/LAZ CRS or CSV plot centres
    --buffer Processing margin Default 5 m; preserve core polygon in metadata

    Important: G:\Survey\BR01_plots.geojson is a placeholder for a real user-supplied plot file, not an observed artifact from this case study.

    3.6.1 Using the author's own field-plot boundaries

    An investigator who already has mapped inventory plots does not need to generate random or continuous plots. The imported mode clips the LiDAR survey using the provided geometries. Supported inputs from the supplied FAST-GIS modules include ESRI Shapefile (.shp plus its .shx, .dbf, and preferably .prj companions), KML, KMZ, GeoJSON, GeoPackage (.gpkg), and CSV plot centres. Preserve unique plot identifiers and a valid coordinate reference system. Polygon inputs define their own core boundaries; CSV centre records need shape dimensions and, when needed, an explicit CRS. Do not interpret a centre-only CSV as an arbitrary polygon boundary.

    Shapefile example (existing polygon boundaries):

    fastgc gis `
    --in_path "G:\ALS_Jin\concurrent\BR01\ALS\ALS-on_BR01_2019-07-05_300m.laz" `
    --out_dir "G:\FASTGC_BR01_SHP_Plots" `
    --sensor_mode ALS `
    --sampling imported `
    --plot_file "G:\Survey\permanent_plots.shp" `
    --buffer 5
    Parameter Function / definition Configuration guidance
    --plot_file Existing SHP polygon dataset Include shapefile companion files and a valid CRS
    --sampling imported Use authored plot geometry Does not resample locations
    --buffer Processing margin around imported core 5 m in this example; preserves polygon as core
    --out_dir Published collection workspace Use a fresh directory
    --in_path, --sensor_mode Source survey and sensor Keep consistent with survey

    KML example (existing plot boundaries):

    fastgc gis `
    --in_path "G:\ALS_Jin\concurrent\BR01\ALS\ALS-on_BR01_2019-07-05_300m.laz" `
    --out_dir "G:\FASTGC_BR01_KML_Plots" `
    --sensor_mode ALS `
    --sampling imported `
    --plot_file "G:\Survey\permanent_plots.kml" `
    --buffer 5
    Parameter Function / definition Configuration guidance
    --plot_file KML boundary dataset Verify KML coordinates and polygon validity
    --sampling imported Honor original plot locations No random plot selection
    --buffer Processing support Here 5 m
    --out_dir Separate output collection Avoid overwriting other sampling demonstrations
    --in_path, --sensor_mode Survey input and sensor type ALS example

    For a multilayer GeoPackage, add --layer "plot_boundaries" to select the appropriate layer. The examples above require the investigator's actual SHP or KML file; these placeholder paths are not part of the supplied BR01 dataset. After importing, run plots-run against the newly generated ALS_plots collection using the same command pattern shown in §3.9.

    3.7 Survey catalog, CRS, multiple source files, and spatial integrity

    The integrated source modules include survey discovery/cataloging, survey-region grouping, spatial access, clipping across compatible source files, and transactional publication. These support single-file and directory-based survey inputs.

    Function / module Role Integrity or performance implication
    survey_catalog.build_survey_catalog Discover LAS/LAZ survey files and CRS/bounds Record skipped/unresolved CRS tiles
    survey_regions / survey_index Group and index survey coverage Support coverage-aware sampling
    spatial_index / las_query Spatial candidate retrieval Reduce unnecessary scanning in relevant operations
    multi_clip.clip_las_sources Clip points from compatible source files Deduplicate overlapping source returns as implemented
    plot_io.import_plots Parse external plot geometry Preserve plot IDs and CRS transformation
    plot_runner.extract_plots Extract core+buffer points and metadata Publish per-plot LAZ and JSON
    publish.publish_directory Stage and publish completed collections Avoid exposing partially written collection roots
    core_mask.mask_plot_rasters_to_core Crop/mask rasters to core geometry Requires correct processing metadata

    Use a projected CRS with metre units for geometric parameters in metres. A nominally identical EPSG code does not always guarantee that differently encoded CRS representations pass the strict comparison used by a particular module.

    3.9 From clipping to downstream plots-run

    The downstream workflow should discover all plots from the collection manifest without manual JSON editing or plot-by-plot commands. It publishes results within the collection root; an unrelated --out_dir is rejected.

    fastgc `
    --workflow plots-run `
    --in_path "G:\FASTGC_BR01_Buffered_Test\ALS_plots" `
    --sensor_mode ALS `
    --products FAST_GC FAST_DEM FAST_CHM `
    --grid_res 0.5 `
    --jobs 1
    Parameter Function / definition Configuration guidance
    --workflow plots-run Managed plot-collection processing Reads plots_manifest.json and per-plot JSON
    --in_path Collection root Must be the ALS_plots directory, not its parent
    --sensor_mode Sensor mode Must be consistent with the collection
    --products Requested products Here ground classification, DEM, CHM
    --grid_res Raster resolution Here 0.5 m; adjust for point density and plot scale
    --jobs Concurrent plot workers Here 1 for reproducible first validation
    --out_dir Omit for managed collection The CLI requires collection-root publication

    3.9.1 Product lineage

    1. FAST-GIS: survey → sampled polygon cores → buffered LAZ + per-plot metadata + collection manifest.
    2. FAST_GC: classify ground/non-ground returns in each plot.
    3. FAST_DEM: interpolate terrain elevation from suitable ground support.
    4. FAST_DSM: estimate surface elevation from appropriate returns when requested.
    5. FAST_CHM: estimate height above terrain; method-dependent surface processing.
    6. FAST_NORMALIZED: express point Z relative to terrain.
    7. FAST_TERRAIN: derive terrain metrics from the DEM.
    8. FAST_STRUCTURE: derive vertical and geometric forest descriptors.
    9. FAST_ITD / FAST_TREECLOUDS: tree detection and individual-tree extraction, when requested and appropriately configured.
    10. FAST_CHANGE: compare matched products from multiple surveys, not meaningful from a single-date plot alone.

    This is a conceptual lineage, not a claim that every downstream product depends only on the three initial rasters or that every product was successfully executed in the demonstrated command.

    3.10 Ground, surface, and canopy-height product bundle

    Product / function Definition Metric and units Utility and cautions
    FAST_GC / ground classification Assign ground vs non-ground classification to LiDAR returns Point class codes, spatial distribution Terrain estimation, vegetation separation; not a raster
    FAST_DEM / digital elevation model Terrain elevation interpolated from ground-classified LiDAR returns. Ground elevation (m) Slope, aspect, normalization; interpolation method matters
    FAST_DSM / digital surface model Elevation of the upper visible surface represented by LiDAR returns. Surface elevation (m) Canopy top, built surface, rough-height proxy
    FAST_CHM / canopy height model Canopy height above the local terrain, derived from surface and terrain elevations. Canopy height (m) Height distribution, tree detection; surface method matters
    FAST_NORMALIZED / height normalization Each point’s elevation expressed relative to the terrain elevation at its horizontal location. Point height above terrain (m) Vertical profiles, understory/canopy structure

    3.11 Terrain bundle: definition, measurement, and interpretation

    Terrain products are derived from the DEM. The descriptions below explain the scientific meaning of each product; exact raster kernels, window sizes, and boundary handling depend on the implementation.

    Metric / CLI name Scientific definition Output Use at 15 m core
    Slope, slope_degrees Steepness of the terrain surface, expressed as an angle in degrees. Degrees Steepness, microtopography, plot comparability
    Aspect, aspect Orientation of maximum downslope gradient Azimuth (degrees) Insolation/exposure, ecological gradients
    Hillshade, hillshade Illumination from slope, aspect, solar azimuth/altitude Relative intensity Terrain visualization
    Roughness, roughness Local variation in elevation, per implementation Terrain heterogeneity Surface complexity; window and units require method check
    Terrain Ruggedness Index (TRI), tri Local elevation difference relative to neighbors Terrain ruggedness Terrain heterogeneity
    Topographic Position Index (TPI), tpi Difference between a cell’s elevation and the mean elevation of its neighborhood. Relative elevation (m) Ridges, depressions, microsites
    Profile curvature Surface curvature in downslope direction Curvature Flow acceleration/erosion tendencies
    Planform curvature Curvature perpendicular to slope Curvature Flow convergence/divergence
    Topographic Wetness Index (TWI), twi Terrain wetness potential combining local slope and upslope contributing area. Index Potential wetness; requires appropriate contributing area
    Flow accumulation Number/area of upslope cells reaching cell Cells or area Drainage analysis; plot-edge artifacts important

    Figure 3.5. Plot 005 slope, aspect, hillshade, roughness, TRI, and TPI. Each panel uses its own legend; compare spatial patterns rather than raw color values between different metrics.

    Small-plot caution: hydrological accumulation, watershed boundaries, stream order, and wetness indices are strongly influenced by the plot boundary. For defensible hydrology, compute these on a larger surrounding DEM and extract the core plot afterward.

    3.16 Summary and transition

    FAST-GIS turns spatially extensive ALS/ULS/TLS surveys into reproducible, machine-readable plot collections. The principal scientific safeguards are coverage-aware selection, explicit projected coordinates, separate core and buffer geometries, complete per-plot provenance, and managed downstream product publication. Plot 005 demonstrates how ground-classified and normalized points can support DEM/DSM/CHM surfaces, terrain metrics, and detailed vertical/geometric forest structure. The next chapter develops the ground-classification algorithm and its sensor-specific behavior in detail.

    3.17.1 Reproducible metric definitions in FAST_STRUCTURE

    Important correction to common interpretations: VCI in this implementation is the Shannon entropy normalized by the logarithm of occupied vertical bins, and multi_return_fraction is computed as the proportion of points with return_number>1. It should not be described as the fraction of pulses that produced multiple returns. canopy_cover is return-based, so it should not be equated with field-measured crown projection without calibration.

    3.17.2 Three-dimensional point-distribution descriptors

    FAST_STRUCTURE computes robust weighted covariance within eligible raster cells. It uses a coordinate median and distance-based robust weights to reduce the influence of unusually distant points. Covariance descriptors summarize the spread of points along three principal spatial directions; they do not by themselves identify biological classes.

    planarity Extent to which points occupy a locally planar arrangement. Planar arrangement [R8]

    anisotropy Strength of directional differences in local point geometry. Directional imbalance [R8]
    omnivariance Combined spread of the local point distribution along three principal directions. Overall covariance-volume scale; units depend on eigenvalue units [R8]
